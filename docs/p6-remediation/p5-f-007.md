## 1. Finding

At the Initiator's final-ACK deadline boundary the Responder can succeed while the Initiator does not. The Initiator writes its complete `INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK` after a live deadline preflight; its absolute (5 min) or inactivity (60 s) deadline is reached before its local send confirmation; it times out with no result and offers its authenticated timeout `CANCEL`. The Responder receives the complete final ACK, verifies it, and returns the only `PairingResult`. The Initiator's following `CANCEL` then reaches R's session after R removed its succeeded run, where it is an unknown route (P3 §11.2) and ends R's connection after R's result.

P5 found no conflicting successful result and no authentication bypass. The only gap was that P3 §9 documented the opposite direction alone (I succeeds, R fails, after a lost final ACK).

## 2. P5.2 deterministic evidence

`windows_tcp::tests::p5_transport_review::p5_f007_final_ack_deadline_boundary_end_to_end` drives both deadlines end to end through two scripted adapters with hand clocks. `ManualClock::advance_after(1, …)` moves the Initiator's clock between the owner preflight reading and the confirmation reading inside one `on_writable` call. The table it prints, re-run unchanged in P6.5 (D is 60 s for inactivity and 300 s for absolute):

| Deadline | Case | Owner check / confirmation | Wire from I | I | R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| both | `BothLive` | D−2 ns / D−1 ns | 144 B (full final ACK) | `Confirmed`, result | `InitiatorFinishAck`, result (agrees with I's) |
| both | `CrossesBeforeConfirm` | D−1 ns / exactly D | 262 B (final ACK + 118 B `CANCEL`) | timeout after the full write, no result, `CANCEL` follows | `InitiatorFinishAck`, **result**, then `SessionProtocolFailure` |
| both | `ExpiredAtCheck` | D / D | 118 B (`CANCEL` only) | timeout before any byte | verified peer `CANCEL` (`Timeout`), no result |
| both | `ExpiredAfterCheck` | D+1 ns / D+1 ns | 118 B (`CANCEL` only) | timeout before any byte | verified peer `CANCEL` (`Timeout`), no result |
| both | `PartialThenCrosses` | 4 B at D−1 ns; next write at D | 4 B prefix | `AbandonedPartialFrame`, connection closed, no result | truncated frame then EOF (`PeerClosed`), no result |

The test asserts that the reverse outcome (I none, R result) occurs exactly in `CrossesBeforeConfirm`, that R's result carries the ceremony identity and I's authenticated bootstrap, that two results agree when both exist, and that I keeps `remaining 9` with no route in every row.

## 4. P6-D-005

Recorded in [decisions.md](decisions.md#p6-d-005--local-completion-and-final-ack-deadline-boundary). The owner selected **option A**: keep the current confirmation-time rule, with no backdating to the last-byte instant. Either side may be the only result holder; both asymmetric directions are valid profile outcomes; two successful results can never conflict; R's success requires an actually verified final ACK; expiry still creates no Initiator success; the timeout `CANCEL` after R's success stays under the current §11.2 routing; a later session failure never revokes a returned result; and nothing changes in the wire, cryptography, result shape, deadlines, accounting, or retry behavior.

## 5. Result semantics

A `PairingResult` proves only that **this endpoint met its local verified completion condition for this exact ceremony**. It does not prove that the peer returned a result, still has live state, received any later message, persisted trust, or committed anything in its application, and it does not prove both endpoints completed atomically.

If both endpoints hold a result, the results are compatible: same `ceremony_identity`, complementary roles, each other's authenticated bootstrap bytes, the same `shared_context`, and the same profile and version. This follows from the completion MACs, which bind the exact ceremony identity, both roles, and the purpose, and from the earlier transcript and bootstrap-MAC checks. F-007 concerns whether a local result exists, never disagreement between two results.

## 6. I-only success

```text
I verifies RESPONDER_FINISH_ACK → writes INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK → confirms while live → I PairingResult
                                       └─ the final ACK is lost before R receives it → R: no result
```

R keeps waiting with its guard and session until its own deadline or a disconnect ends the run with no result. This direction was already in P3 §9 and is evidenced by `ceremony::tests::final_ack_lost_after_send_leaves_only_the_initiator_with_a_local_result`.

## 7. R-only success

```text
I verifies RESPONDER_FINISH_ACK → preflight live → complete INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK written
  → I's deadline reached → confirmation check: expired → I times out, no result, CANCEL retained
R receives the complete final ACK → verifies it while R is live → R PairingResult
```

**Why R's success is valid.** R succeeds only after verifying I's `BOOTSTRAP_MAC` (I's authenticated approval of this ceremony), giving its own local approval, verifying `INITIATOR_FINISH`, sending `RESPONDER_FINISH_ACK`, and verifying `INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK`. That final MAC is keyed by the established SAS and bound to `ceremony_identity`, both roles, the purpose `initiator-finish-ack`, and the transcript; only I could have produced it, and only after verifying R's `RESPONDER_FINISH_ACK`. I's deadline expiring an instant later changes I's own local observation, not the authenticity of bytes I already constructed and sent. No conflicting peer authentication arises.

**Why I's failure stays conservative.** A returned socket write is not success. The local ceremony deadline stays authoritative at confirmation: `now >= deadline` there means no Initiator result, no later result, and no "probably sent" success.

## 8. Exact deadline boundary

Expiry stays `now >= deadline` for the 5-minute absolute and the 60-second inactivity deadlines; neither duration changes and the final ACK earns no grace period or extension.

| Preflight | Confirmation | Final ACK | I result | R result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D−2 ns | D−1 ns | complete | yes | yes (compatible) |
| D−1 ns | exactly D | complete, then I's `CANCEL` | **no** | **yes**, then R's session ends on the `CANCEL` |
| D | D (no write) | no byte; `CANCEL` instead | no | no (verified peer `CANCEL`) |
| D+1 ns | D+1 ns (no write) | no byte; `CANCEL` instead | no | no (verified peer `CANCEL`) |
| 4 B at D−1 ns | next write at D: preflight expired | 4-byte prefix; I closes (`AbandonedPartialFrame`) | no | no (truncated frame, EOF) |

All rows hold for both deadlines.

## 9. Timeout CANCEL after R success

- **I after crossing:** the run is terminal (timeout), the guard is released, the opportunity stays consumed, and the authenticated timeout `CANCEL` occupies the now-free output slot and is written after the final ACK.
- **R before that `CANCEL`:** frames on one stream are dispatched in order, so R verifies the final ACK first, succeeds, returns its result, and removes the succeeded run's route.
- **Unknown or stale route:** I's `CANCEL` is structurally routable and names a key with no run on R's live session. Under P3 §11.2 that is session-fatal: the session stops admitting work, every run bound to it (siblings included) fails terminally with no result and no refund, and R's connection closes (`SessionProtocolFailure` in the evidence).
- **Accepted, not special-cased.** P6-D-005 accepts this robustness consequence. P6.5 adds no completed-run tombstone, `CANCEL` grace routing, silent ignore, or rollback.
- **No revocation.** R's result was returned before the `CANCEL` arrived; nothing later alters or revokes it. The session failure affects only state that was still live on that session.

This is a robustness consequence of the existing routing rule, not an authentication contradiction.

## 10. Consumer obligations

- Treat `PairingResult` as local verified completion of one exact ceremony by this endpoint.
- Tolerate either endpoint being the only result holder, in both directions.
- Do not treat a result as a distributed transaction commit, proof of peer success, or proof of durable peer trust. Durable storage and reconciliation are outside this remote ceremony profile.
- Do not expect the protocol to repair an asymmetric outcome. There is no resume, re-send after terminal state, reused approval, or reused ephemeral material; if the consumer wants another attempt, it starts a new ceremony under the normal rules (fresh local authorization, fresh exposure, a new opportunity). P6.5 defines no product retry UX.
- Do not rely on a later disconnect or session failure to mean the result is invalid: a returned result is never revoked.

## 11. Rejected alternative: last-byte timestamp

Option B would record when the final ACK's last byte was written and let a later confirmation succeed if that instant preceded the deadline; the P5.2 crossing row would then read "both results". It was not selected because it:

- introduces a new authoritative transport timestamp that must be trusted through later scheduling;
- permits creating a result after the ceremony is already locally expired, changing what deadline expiry means;
- complicates terminal irreversibility (I1) and would need a broader re-review of terminal-state invariants;
- fixes no authentication defect;
- only moves which endpoint observes success in a protocol that is already inherently asymmetric (a lost final ACK yields I-only success regardless).

Option B is not considered insecure. It is simply not the selected semantic.

## 12. P3 changes

Clarifications of the [P3 profile](../p3-vodozemac-ceremony-profile-draft.md) that make current behavior explicit; no protocol behavior changes:

- **§9, Initiator result condition:** "sent `INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK`" now means the complete frame was accepted by its local send operation and I confirmed that send while the ceremony was still live; the confirmation is judged when it is made, never backdated.
- **§9, result paragraph:** either participant may be the only result holder; I-only (transport loss) and R-only (I's confirmation/deadline boundary) are both described; neither permits conflicting results; a result never proves the peer returned one; there is no retry or repair (a new ceremony is needed); a later disconnect, unknown-route session failure, `CANCEL`, or teardown does not revoke a returned result; consumers must tolerate either side being the only holder and must not treat a result as a distributed commit. Cryptographic statements are unchanged.
- **§10, `AwaitResponderFinish` (I):** produce and write `INITIATOR_FINISH_ACK` with no result yet; only once the complete send is confirmed while live → result, `Succeeded`; expiry at confirmation → `Failed` (timeout), no result, even after a complete write; a partial or failed write is never confirmed.
- **§10, `AwaitInitiatorFinishAck` (R):** R's result depends only on its own live ceremony and the verified complete final ACK, independently of whether I returned its own result.
- **§11.3:** expiry at `elapsed >= deadline`; a complete final-ACK write does not exempt the Initiator from the deadline check its success confirmation requires, extends nothing, earns no grace period, and no earlier transport event creates success after expiry.

## 17. Final disposition

**P5-F-007 → DISPOSITIONED-IN-P6 (P6.5) — current conservative completion boundary retained and documented; no production change required.**
